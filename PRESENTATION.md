## Q1 · What is the only volcano in India?

**1.** **Barren Island**, in the Andaman Sea, is the only volcano in India confirmed
to be actively erupting.
**2.** It lies ~135 km NE of Port Blair (Andaman & Nicobar Islands), at about
12.28°N 93.86°E, in the middle of the Andaman Sea.
**3.** It is the only volcano in India recorded to have erupted in modern history —
and the only one on the entire Sumatra-to-Myanmar volcanic arc to do so.
**4.** It is uninhabited and a restricted protected area, rising just **354 m** above
the waves.

## Q2 · Define the volcano and its surroundings

**5.** A **volcano** is an opening in Earth's crust where magma, ash and gases erupt.
Barren Island's is a **stratovolcano** set inside a broad **caldera**.
**6.** The island itself is only ~**3 km** across — the rim of a caldera ringed by
**250–350 m** walls.
**7.** Inside sits a ~**2 km**-wide caldera, breached open to the sea on the west,
holding a young pyroclastic cone with two small craters.
**8.** The whole edifice rises ~**2,250 m** from the seabed; only its tip pokes out,
built from **basalt and basaltic andesite**.

## Q3 · Its last eruption and why

**9.** Its most recent confirmed eruption ran **30 Jul 2025 – Jan 2026**; an earlier
large episode (**15 Mar – 29 May 2024**) ranks among the biggest ever recorded.
**10.** **WHY (plates):** the Indian plate subducts under the Burma microplate; water
dragged down lowers the mantle's melting point and melts rock into magma.
**11.** **WHY (magma):** the buoyant melt rises through cracks into a magma chamber,
and expanding trapped gas drives the Strombolian explosions and fire fountains.
**12.** **WHAT the show is:** small but frequent — mostly **VEI 1–2**, **20+ events
since 1900** — ash plumes, lava fountains, and lava flows that reach the caldera
breach and the sea.

## Q4 · What type is it {Cinder, Shield or Composite} and {Active, Dormant or Extinct}?

**13.** By form it is a **COMPOSITE (stratovolcano)** — not a cinder-cone or a shield.
**14.** It is built from alternating layers of lava, ash and pyroclastic debris, and
nested in a caldera formed by a **late-Pleistocene collapse**.
**15.** By activity it is **ACTIVE** — not dormant or extinct — reawakening in
**1991** after ~150 years asleep and still monitored as in eruption.

## Cheat sheet (say this in 60 seconds)

Barren Island is India's **only active volcano** (1). It sits **135 km NE of Port
Blair** in the Andaman Sea (2), is the **only Indian volcano to erupt in recorded
history** (3) and rises just **354 m** (4). A volcano is a **vent where magma, ash
and gas erupt** (5); Barren Island's is a **stratovolcano in a caldera** (5) — an
island **3 km** wide (6) with a **2 km** caldera open to the sea (7), rising
**2,250 m** from the seabed (8). Its latest confirmed eruption was **Jul 2025–Jan
2026**, with a big one in **2024** (9), because the **Indian plate subducts under
the Burma plate** (10), melting rock into magma that rises into a chamber and
**builds gas pressure** (11) — producing **small but frequent** Strombolian bursts
(12). By form it's a **COMPOSITE / stratovolcano** (13), built from **layered lava
and ash** inside a **late-Pleistocene caldera** (14), and by state it is
**ACTIVE** (15).
